# How to Negotiate Your Salary in the UAE Without Losing the Offer

Most job seekers in the UAE accept the first offer they are given — not because it is a good offer, but because they are afraid that asking for more will make the offer disappear. That fear costs real money over a two-year contract. Negotiating is normal, expected, and safe, as long as you do it the right way.

## Know the market rate before you talk numbers

Never negotiate from a feeling. Before you reply to an offer, check what your role actually pays across the UAE — the same job pays differently in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, and differently again between a large company and a small one. A number backed by real market data is a reason; a number pulled from hope is a guess, and employers can tell the difference.

## Negotiate the package, not just the basic salary

A UAE salary is a package, and the split matters as much as the total. Your end-of-service gratuity is calculated on your basic salary, not on the total package — so an offer with a higher basic and a smaller housing allowance can be worth more over two years than an offer with a bigger headline total. Ask for the full breakdown in writing, then compare the basic, the housing allowance, transport, annual flights and medical cover line by line.

## Ask about the things that cost you money later

Salary is only one line in the contract. Ask whether annual flights cover your family or just you, whether the 30 days of annual leave can be carried over, which hospitals are on the medical network, and who pays the visa and work permit costs. An employer should pay for your work visa and labour card — if an offer asks you to pay those costs yourself, that is a warning sign, not a normal term.

## How to ask without sounding difficult

Keep it simple and professional. Thank them for the offer, name the one or two points you would like to discuss, and give a reason tied to the market or the role — never to your personal expenses. Ask once, clearly, and then let them answer. Do not bluff about another offer you do not have, and do not threaten to walk away unless you genuinely will. Employers respect a candidate who knows their worth and states it calmly; they remember the candidate who played games.

## Get the final offer in writing

Whatever you agree, make sure the written offer matches it — every allowance, every figure, every promise. A verbal promise about a raise after probation or a flight for your family is worth nothing if the contract says something else. Read the contract before you sign, and check the numbers against the market rate one last time.
