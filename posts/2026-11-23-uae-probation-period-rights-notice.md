# UAE Probation Period: Your Salary, Notice and Rights in the First Six Months

Almost every UAE job starts with probation, and most people sign the contract without reading what probation actually means for their pay and their exit options. The rules sit in the UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021), and they're more specific than most new hires realise.

## How long can probation last?

Six months, maximum. Your employer can't extend it, and you can only serve probation once with the same employer — if they rehire you later, they can't put you back on a fresh probation for the same role. The period counts toward your total service, so it feeds into your end-of-service gratuity from day one.

## Do you get your full salary during probation?

Yes. Probation changes your job security, not your pay. The salary in your contract is the salary you're owed, paid through the Wage Protection System like everyone else's. An employer who offers a "probation salary" lower than the contract figure is breaking the agreement you both signed. Annual leave is the one area that works differently: you can take accrued leave during probation only if the employer agrees, and if you resign before using it, those days generally aren't paid out the way they are after probation.

## What notice do you owe if you want to leave?

It depends where you're going:

- **Joining another employer in the UAE:** one month's written notice. Your new employer may also have to reimburse your current one for recruitment costs — that's between the two companies, but it explains why some offers move slowly.
- **Leaving the UAE:** 14 days' written notice.
- **If your employer ends your contract during probation:** they owe you 14 days' written notice.

Miss the notice rules and the law allows compensation claims tied to the notice period, so the resignation letter matters as much as the decision.

## Why this matters before you sign

Probation is when your bargaining power is at its lowest and your costs — relocation, housing deposits, family visas — are at their highest. Read the probation clause, the notice clause and the salary breakdown together before you accept, not after the first payslip surprises you.
